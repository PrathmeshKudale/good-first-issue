Detailed Process Stages

01

Cane Preparation & Milling

Mechanical harvesters cut cane, transported to factory, weighed, chopped by fibrizer, crushed in 3-5 roller mills with imbibition water addition for maximum sucrose recovery.

02

Juice Clarification (Sulphitation)

Raw juice heated to 95°C, lime (CaO) and SO₂ added for pH adjustment, precipitates impurities as "mud," filtered to produce clear juice.

03

Evaporation & Concentration

4-5 effect evaporators concentrate juice from 15% to 65% solids (syrup), using steam economy and vapor bleeding for process heating.

Crystallization & Recovery

04

Vacuum Pan Boiling

Syrup boiled under vacuum (low temperature) to prevent caramelization, seeded with sugar fines to initiate crystal growth, forming massecuite.

05

Crystallization Stages

A-Massecuite → White Sugar (VHP), B-Massecuite → Remelt/Seed, C-Massecuite → Final Molasses. Three boiling system maximizes sucrose recovery.

06

Centrifugation & Drying

High-speed centrifuges (1200-1500 RPM) separate crystals from molasses, washed, dried in fluidized bed dryers, graded by size, packaged.

Material Handling: Automated conveyor systems, forklift operations, AGV transport, and strict FIFO (First In-First Out) protocols ensure efficient workflow from raw material intake to finished goods dispatch.

Safety Procedures & Protective Systems

Safety Procedures Framework

🔥

Fire Safety

Fire extinguishers, sprinklers, hydrants installed. Regular fire drills and evacuation practices. Bagasse storage fire prevention protocols.

⚡

Electrical & Mechanical

Earthing and insulation checks, periodic machine inspection, preventive maintenance schedules, LOTO procedures.

Proper labeling and storage of lime, SO₂, molasses. Ventilation and fume extraction systems. Dust explosion prevention.

CHAPTER 9

Challenging Experiences & Insights

Challenges Faced

Scale of Operations

Understanding synchronization between Mill, Boiler, Process House, and Turbine departments required intense observation and cross-functional learning.

High-Risk Environments

Working near high-pressure steam (45-110 kg/cm²), hot surfaces, and heavy rotating machinery demanded strict safety awareness.

Troubleshooting Pressure

Witnessing conveyor belt breakdowns and centrifuge faults required understanding rapid fault diagnosis under production pressure.

Environmental Adaptation

Adjusting to factory noise, shift timings, and standing for long observation periods was physically demanding initially.

Positive Experiences

Mechanical Maintenance

Directly aligned with Mechanical Engineering diploma—exposure to turbines, gearboxes, boilers, and pumps was highly engaging.

Teamwork Observation

Coordination between departments during shift changes and emergencies demonstrated professional industrial culture.

By-Product Utilization

Learning waste-to-wealth concepts—bagasse to power, molasses to ethanol, press mud to fertilizer—showcased circular economy.

Areas for Improvement

  • • Effluent treatment and molasses handling odors
  • • Ergonomic improvements for long standing operations
  • • Noise reduction in boiler sections
